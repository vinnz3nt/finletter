Week ending Friday, September 4, 2026

This week in the global economy

  • Global sovereign bonds sold off sharply, with Japan's 10-year yield hitting 3% for the first time since 1996, the 10-year gilt topping 5.2% and France's 10-year reaching 4.21%, its highest since 2008, as the yen slid toward the 160-per-dollar intervention threshold.
  • Renewed US–Iran strikes around the Strait of Hormuz drove Brent crude to roughly $96 a barrel, a six-week high and a gain of more than 7% on the week.
  • Eurozone inflation accelerated to 3.3% in August, its highest since September 2023 on surging energy costs, leaving markets pricing a quarter-point ECB hike to 2.50% at the September 10 meeting.
  • Fed Governor Christopher Waller signaled openness to holding rates steady this month, cutting September hike odds to about 50% and sparking a rebound that lifted the Dow more than 600 points on Thursday, with August payrolls (consensus roughly 55,000 after July's 23,000 loss) due Friday.
  • China's Caixin manufacturing PMI rose to a two-month high of 51.5 in August with export orders growing at their fastest pace in six months, though business confidence fell to its weakest since January on soft domestic demand.
